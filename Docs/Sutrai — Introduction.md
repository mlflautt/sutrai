# Sutrai

**Sutrai** — *Sutra + AI*, and phonetically *sutra + eye* — is an experimental archive and creative system for producing faithful, beautiful audiovisual representations of enduring sacred, religious, philosophical, and folk stories.

Its simplest aim is:

> **Take an old story worth remembering, understand it carefully, and give it new audiovisual form.**

The project begins from the belief that ancient stories, parables, myths, teachings, visions, and sacred narratives remain powerful creative material. Many have survived for centuries precisely because they can be encountered repeatedly and still yield meaning. Sutrai explores how contemporary generative tools can help these stories enter new media without reducing them to generic “content.”

The primary creative trajectory is:

**Source → Story → Interpretation → Concept Art → Storyboard → Animated Sequence → Sound / Music / Voice → Edit → Finished Film**

The long-term goal is to make much of this process **agentically executable**: an AI agent should eventually be able to select or receive a story, retrieve its sources and relevant context, develop a defensible interpretation, visualize its world, create shots, generate media, assemble an edit, and produce a polished short-form audiovisual work while retaining a traceable relationship to the source material.

Sutrai itself is not intended to contain every generative technology required to accomplish this. Image, animation, music, sound, voice, and video-generation workflows may be developed in specialized repositories and tools such as ComfyUI, while editing and finishing systems may be developed around applications such as DaVinci Resolve. **This repository is the conceptual and archival center of the system:** the place where source texts, extracted stories, creative principles, research, project specifications, and individual adaptation projects are organized.

## The Archive

A central foundation of Sutrai is a **local-first source library**.

Primary texts, translations, commentaries, folklore collections, scholarly works, and related material should remain available as the evidentiary layer beneath generated interpretations. AI-generated summaries and annotations are useful, but they should remain secondary to identifiable sources.

Where possible, the archive should preserve:

- original-language or source-text editions alongside translations;
- multiple translations for comparison;
- bibliographic and provenance information;
- extracted individual stories, parables, teachings, hymns, dialogues, and episodes;
- relationships between related stories, motifs, figures, symbols, and concepts;
- distinctions between source text, later commentary, modern interpretation, and creative adaptation.

A large scripture or mythology collection is therefore not merely stored as a monolithic document. Sutrai should gradually make its **individual narrative and conceptual units addressable**. A single parable, episode, vision, or teaching can become a reusable research object — and potentially the nucleus of a film.

This also creates a foundation for comparative research. Similar structures may be studied across cultures without assuming that they are identical: flood myths, sacred mountains, divine messengers, journeys into other worlds, transformation, death and rebirth, emptiness, illumination, sacrifice, hidden knowledge, sacred speech, the relationship between human beings and the cosmos, and countless other recurring forms.

## Agentic Creative Practice

Sutrai is intended to become an environment in which AI agents can acquire **reliable creative and archival skills**, not merely issue isolated generations.

Examples might include:

- `extract_story`
- `compare_translations`
- `trace_sources`
- `research_context`
- `find_related_motifs`
- `develop_adaptation`
- `create_visual_bible`
- `build_storyboard`
- `generate_shot_manifest`
- `prepare_voice_and_music_plan`
- `assemble_edit`
- `verify_source_fidelity`

These workflows should be modular, documented, reproducible, and designed so that different capable agents can enter the repository and perform useful work without damaging its structure.

Over time, successful creative procedures can become reusable paths of execution: not a single monolithic “make movie” button, but a growing repertoire of tested skills.

## Creative Principle

The archival side of Sutrai should be careful, scholarly, and transparent.

The creative side may be **visionary**.

Adaptations can be cinematic, mystical, symbolic, dreamlike, devotional, austere, psychedelic, Hermetic, alchemical, folkloric, cosmic, or radically beautiful when the material supports such treatment. Fidelity does not require visual conservatism. It requires understanding what is being represented, knowing where interpretation begins, and approaching the source in good faith.

Sutrai therefore aims to occupy an unusual place in contemporary generative media: a small **bastion of deliberate, timeless storytelling** amid platforms optimized for disposable content.

The intended destinations may include short films, visual scriptures, animated parables, music-driven interpretations, experimental religious cinema, YouTube works, TikTok and Instagram pieces, installations, web experiences, and formats not yet imagined.

The same archive may also support quieter applications: semantic exploration of sacred canons, comparative study, source-grounded questions, guided discovery, or something as simple as a daily passage delivered to a small physical display.

## Direction

Sutrai may eventually become many things.

For now, its center is intentionally simple:

**Build a trustworthy archive of enduring stories.  
Teach agents how to understand and work with them.  
Create beautiful audiovisual representations worth passing on.**
