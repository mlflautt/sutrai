# Motif Index — Self-Sacrifice for Others

## self-sacrifice-for-others

### Description

A character voluntarily offers their own life, safety, or well-being for the benefit of others. This motif embodies the highest ideal of altruism and servant leadership.

### Cross-Cultural Distribution

- **Buddhist** — Jataka tales: the bodhisattva ideal of sacrificing oneself for all beings
- **Christian** — Christ's sacrifice: "Greater love hath no man than this, that a man lay down his life for his friends"
- **Greek** — Prometheus: suffers eternally for giving fire to humanity
- **Norse** — Odin: sacrifices himself to himself on Yggdrasil to gain wisdom
- **Secular** — The soldier who throws himself on a grenade to save his comrades

### Cultural Context

Self-sacrifice for others represents the pinnacle of moral action across cultures. It is the foundation of the bodhisattva ideal in Buddhism, the Christ ideal in Christianity, and the heroic ideal in virtually all warrior traditions. The motif transforms death from defeat into victory, from ending into beginning.

### Adaptation Notes

Self-sacrifice creates the most emotionally powerful moments in narrative. The Monkey King's bridge-of-bodies and the Banyan Deer's voluntary death both create iconic visual moments. These scenes work because they combine visual clarity with moral depth—the audience understands immediately what is being given up and why.
