# Motif Index — Cosmic Sacrifice

## cosmic-sacrifice

### Description

A primordial being (god, giant, or cosmic entity) is slain and its body is used to create the world. The cosmos is literally formed from the physical remains of this sacrificed being.

### Thompson Motif-Index Reference

### Cross-Cultural Distribution

- **Norse** — Ymer (or Ymir): Odin and his brothers slay the giant and fashion the world from his body
- **Mesopotamian** — Tiamat: Marduk splits her body in two, creating sky and earth
- **Chinese** — Pangu: His body becomes the features of the earth after his death
- **Hindu** — Purusha: The cosmic man is sacrificed by the gods; the world and social order are formed from his body

### Cultural Context

The cosmic sacrifice motif embodies the idea that creation requires destruction, that life emerges from death, and that the physical world is itself a transformed body. It establishes a profound connection between the material world and a living, sacrificed being—the earth is not mere matter but transformed life.

### Adaptation Notes

The cosmic sacrifice offers visceral, transformative imagery: a giant's body becoming the world. The theme of creation-through-violence, of the world as a body, provides both visual power and philosophical depth. The Norse version is particularly striking with its systematic mapping of body parts to world features.
