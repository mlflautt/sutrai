# Vii Agni 107

**Source:** Hymns Of The Samaveda (Internet Archive (unmapped))
**Lines:** 9739-9797

VII.  Agni. 

THY  glories  are,  like  lightnings  from  the  rainy  cloud, 
visible,  Agai,  like  the  comings  of  the  Dawns, 

When,  loosed  to  wander  over  plants  and  forest  trees, 
thou  crammest  by  thyself  thy  food  into  thy  mouth. 

2  When,  sped  and  urged  by  wind,  thon  spreadest  thee 

abroad,  soon  piercing  through  thy  food  according 
to  thy  will, 

The  hosts,  who  ne'er decayest, eager  to  consume, 
like  men  on  chariots,  Agni  !  strive  on  every  side. 

3  Agni, the Hotar-priest  who  fills  the  assembly  full, 

waker  of  wisdom,  chief  controller  of  the  thought— 

Thee, yea, none other than  thyself,  doth  man  elect 
priest  of  the  holy  offering,  great  and  small,  alike. 

VIII  Mitra-Varuna. 

EVEN  far and wide,  O  Varuna  and  Mitra,  doth  your 

help  extend: 

May I  obtain  your  kind  good-will ! 
2  True Gods, may we  completely  gain food and  a 

dwelling-place  from  you  : 
Ye Mitras, may we  be  your  own  !
