# 3 For The Future Tense A Prefix A 53

**Source:** The Ewe-Speaking Peoples of the Slave Coast of West Africa (African)
**Lines:** 8720-8793

3. For the future tense—a prefix a. 

4, For the past tense—a prefix e. 

Example— 
du, to eat. 
Present Tense. 
me du, I eat. mi du, we eat. 
wo du, thou eatest. mi du, you eat. 
e du, he eats. o du, they eat. 
Imperfect Tense. 
me dua, I was eating. mi dua, we were eating. 
wo dua, thou wast eating. mi dua, you were eating. 
e dua, he was eating. o dua, they were eating. 
Future Tense. 
me adu, I will eat. mi adu, we will eat. 
wo adu, thou wilt eat. mi adu, you will eat. 
e adu, he will eat. o adu, they will eat. 
Past Tense. 
me edu, I ate. mi edu, we ate. 
wo edu, thou atest. mi edu, you ate. 

e edu, he ate. o edu, they ate. 

234 THE EWE-SPEAKING PEOPLES. 

In the first and second person singular of the future 
tense, the vowel of the pronoun is commonly elided 
before the prefix a of the verb. Thus, m’adu, 
wadu. 

As in Tshi and Ga, verbs are also conjugated nega- 
tively. In the negative conjugation, three of the 
pronouns are used -in their independent form,! instead 
of as above ; nye, I, being substituted for me; mao, we, 
for mz; and mia, you, for mi. Tenses are formed as 
when the verb is conjugated positively, and the negative 
is indicated by a prefix me, and a suffix o. Thus the 
negative conjugation of du, to eat, is— 

me-du-o, not to eat.
