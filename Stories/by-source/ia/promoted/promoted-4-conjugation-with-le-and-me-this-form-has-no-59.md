# 4 Conjugation With Le And Me This Form Has No 59

**Source:** The Ewe-Speaking Peoples of the Slave Coast of West Africa (African)
**Lines:** 8877-8956

4. Conjugation with le and me. This form has no 
imperfect and no future tense. 

Example— 
le-du-m’, to continue to eat. 
Present Tense. Past Tense. 
me le-du-w me ele-du-m’ 
wo le-du-mWw wo ele-du-m’ 
&e. &e. 

This is also conjugated negatively. 
me-le-du-m’-o, not to continue to eat. 

Present Tense. Past Tense. 
nye me-le-du-m’-o nye me-ele-du-m’-o 
wo me-le-du-m’-o wo me-ele-du-m’-o 

&e. &e. 

In conjugations (3) and (4), where the verb is already 
a compound one, the words forming it are transposed. 
Thus— 

dia nu, to challenge. 

le-nu-dia-ge, to intend to challenge. 
And where the verb is one of strengthened or re-

duplicated root-form, it is usual to place the simple

I taste, me due (from du, to eat). 
I intend to taste, me le-du-due-ge. 

In Ewe, some verbs are both objective and subjective, 
thus— 
yt, to go, means also, to go to. 

Some denote a quality, as— 
Ju, to be white. 
sé, to be hard. 
vé, to be bitter. 

Others are both transitive and intransitive, as— 
kpa, to break, or to be broken. 
kla, to separate, or to be separated. 
bu, to lose, or to be lost. 

There are not many objective verbs. This is remedied 
by verbs being both transitive and intransitive, and by 
the use of compound verbs. 

ARTICLES. 

There is no indefinite article in Ewe. The definite 
article is la. It is derived from Je, to be in a place, and 
its original form was lea, which by use has become la, 
or a. 

In the form /a it is suffixed to verbs, which then 
become nouns expressing quality or office. In the form 
a it is suffxed to nouns to give the meaning “the”. 
Thus— 

nyonu, wife. nyonua, the wife. 
awe, house. awea, or awa, the house. 
ge, root. gea, the root.
