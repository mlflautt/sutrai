# I Wu This God Is The Ocean Fie Junior Of The 26

**Source:** The Ewe-Speaking Peoples of the Slave Coast of West Africa (African)
**Lines:** 2493-2560

I. Wu.—This god is the Ocean, fie junior of the 
three principal ae formerly worshipped in the old 
kingdom of Whydah, and whose worship has since 
spread into Dahomi. His name is generally spelt Hu, 
but the proper pronunciation is with the aspirated w 
(hw)—wu. All along the western portion of the Slave 
Coast, the natives, like those of the Gold Coast, see in 
the roar and motion of the surf a multitude of gods, 
each of whom is worshipped in his own proper locality, 
so that there are two or three local sea-gods to almost 
every village along the shore; but at Whydah and its 
neh boutiond mn is not ees case, and Wu appears to 
be egfotlone the god of the sea generally, and not of 
some portion only. 

Although Wu was the junior of the old Whydah triad, 
at the present day his chief priest, the Wu-no, takes 
precedence of all other priests in Whydah. When the 
surf, which is nearly always so bad as to render landing 
difficult even in surf-boats, becomes so bad as to stop all 
commerce, the Wu-no goes in procession to the beach, 
and invokes the sea to be calm. “Oh, Wu! Wu, the
