within  the  narrowest  limits  both  of  time  and  of  power  a 
reign  which,  so  long  as  it  lasted,  necessarily  encroached 
upon  and  indeed  superseded  his  own.1  What  became  of 
the  divine  king's  female  partner,  the  human  goddess  who 
shared  his  bed  and  transmitted  his  beneficent  energies  to 
the  rest  of  nature,  we  cannot  say.  So  far  as  I  am  aware, 
there  is  little  or  no  evidence  that  she  like  him  suffered 
death  when  her  primary  function  was  discharged.2  The 
nature  of  maternity  suggests  an  obvious  reason  for  sparing 
her  a  little  longer,  till  that  mysterious  law,  which  links 
together  woman's  life  with  the  changing  aspects  of  the 
nightly  sky,  had  been  fulfilled  by  the  birth  of  an  infant 
god,  who  should  in  his  turn,  reared  perhaps  by  her  tender 
care,  grow  up  to  live  and  die  for  the  world.
