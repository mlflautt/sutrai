# 2 Relative Pronouns 61

**Source:** The Ewe-Speaking Peoples of the Slave Coast of West Africa (African)
**Lines:** 9014-9037

2. Relative Pronouns. 

These in the simple form are he and ke. He seems 
to be the verb he, to have or hold, and ke the verb ke, 
to separate. They are suffixed to ame, man or person, 
when a person is referred to, and to nu, thing, when a 
thing is referred to. Thus— 

ameke, who, 1. e. the person who (literally, the person 
separated). 
nuke, whieh, 1. e. the thing which. 

The adverbial relative pronouns of place, time, and 
manner are similarly formed by adding he and ke to 
certain other words, as follows— 

Sihe, or fike, where (Afi, place). 
gbe-ke-gbe, or gbe-he-gbe, when (Gbe, to-day, the day).
