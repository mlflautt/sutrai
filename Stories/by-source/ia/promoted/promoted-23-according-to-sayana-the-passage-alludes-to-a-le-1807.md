# 23 According To Sayana The Passage Alludes To A Le 1807

**Source:** Htst Airp Swirtc (Internet Archive (unmapped))
**Lines:** 14228-14248

23. According to Sayana , the passage alludes to a legend 
of Vishwamitra having been seized and bound by the followers 
of Vasishtha , when observing a vow of silence these were his 
reflections on the occasion ; disparaging the rivalry of 
Vasishtha with himself, as if between an ass and a horse : 
some of the terms are unusual ; sayakasya, commonly, of 
an arrow, is explained agreeably to its etymology. from so t 
to destroy, avasanakarinah. of the finisher or destroyer, 
that is, of Vishwamitra : Lodham nay anti, they lead the sage, 
deriving lodha from lubdham. desirous that his penance might 
not ho frustrated, tapasahkshayo mahhud iti, lohhena lushnim 
sthitam Rishim pashum manyainana, thinking the Rishi silent 
through hi** desire, to be an animal. i.e., stupid. Yaska 
interprets the phrase in the same manner, Lubdham rishim 
nayanti pashum manyamamh , they take away the desiring 
Rishi , thinking him an animal: in the second half the words 
are also of unusual application : na avajinam vajina hasayanti : 
Sayana derives vajina from vach, speed, with ina aflix, and 
interprets it sarvajna , all-knowing ; the contrary, a vajina, by 
murk ha, a fool.
