ment. — Samndrad-mmir-madhuman udarat : the meaning, 
according to the Scholiast, varies greatly according to the 
signification given to samudm ; as, samodante asmin yaga-

samudrad-dravantiajjah. that from which waters rush, celestial 
fire, lightning : urmi in the first case may imply reward or 
consequence, in the latter, rain ; and in the latter sense of 
urmi, samudra may have one of its more ordinary meanings r

also made to apply to other objects of the hymn ; as, for 
instance, samudra may imply, etymologically, the udder of 
the cow, whence flows milk, samudravati, from w T hich come 
urmi, ghi or butter: the whole hymn occurs in the Yajush, 
17. 89—99, where Mahidkara limits the objects to two, Ohi 
and Agni, atra annadhyasena ghritam stuyate jmvmdhyasena
