These fragments, I was afterwards told, consisted of three pieces 
each of the breast, liver, lungs, kidneys, intestines, stomach, 
hindleg, foreleg, neck, and root of the tail. The Queen Mother 
now lifted the pieces out of the ktidiio with her brass spoon and 
placed some in each pot, on the stone, and in the small hole 
she had scraped out. A small piece of the breast of the sacrifice 
was now dipped into the hole by the Queen Mother, taken out 
again, and handed to the herald to carry home for the chief. 
It was put into the hole to get ‘ the spirit *, I was informed. 
The remainder of the sacrifice was divided up as follows : 

{a) The Queen Mother, foreleg, head, and skin. 

{b) Chief and elders, hindlegs, the other foreleg. 

(c) The ‘ linguist ’ (spokesman), the testicles. 

The small pieces remaining in the kuduo the Queen Mother 
and her household took home to eat. The offerings in the 
little pots had, I was informed, disappeared by the following 
day. The white cloths were left upon the trees. There was 
a certain spot near the grove for throwing away the contents 
of the intestines of the sacrifice; the old woman, who had 
carried the kudiw from the vills^e, showed the men where this 
was. On her return she demanded a piece of meat, and the 
photograph (Fig. 44) shows her in the act of passing a knife 
to some one who is to cut her a slice. 

The above completed the ceremonies at the grove.
