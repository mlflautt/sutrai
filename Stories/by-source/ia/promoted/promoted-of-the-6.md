GOLD  COAST  OF  WEST  AFRICA. 

CHAPTEK I. 

INTRODUCTORY. 

The  Gold  Coast  consists  of  that  portion  of  the  West 
African  coast  comprised  between  the  Assini  Eiver  on 
the  west  and  the  Volta  River  on  the  east,  and  extends 
from  about  3°  30'  west  longitude  to  30'  east  longitude. 
The  Gold  Coast  colony  extends  from  the  Assini  Eiver 
to  the  town  of  Flohow,  some  sixty  miles  to  the,  east  of 
the  Volta  Eiver  ;  but  the  territory  beyond  the  latter  is 
really  a  portion  of  the  Slave  Coast, 

The  greater  portion  of  the  Gold  Coast  consists  of 
dense  forest,  which  covers  innumerable  small  hills, 
averaging  from  100  to  300  feet  in  height.  Inland, 
these  hills  take  the  shape  of  low  ranges  running 
more  or  less  parallel  with  the  general  line  of  the  coast, 
ahd  which  culminate  in  the  well-known  Adansi  Hills. 
All  this  forest  tract,  locally  termed  "the  bush,"  may, 
for  all  practicable  purposes,  be  considered  impenetrable.
